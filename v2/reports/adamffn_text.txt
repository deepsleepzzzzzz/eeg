ArticleAdaptive Domain-Aligned Multi-Modal Feature Fusion Networkfor Cross-Speed Fault Diagnosis of Planetary Gearboxes Xin Xia 1, * and Xiaolu Wang 2 1 School of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering, Suqian University, Suqian 223800, China 2 Information Construction Center, Suqian University, Suqian 223800, China; wangxiaolu@squ.edu.cn * Correspondence: xiaxin@alumni.hust.edu.cn AbstractVibration signals of planetary gearboxes under variable-speed conditions exhibit strongnon-stationarity and modulation, so a single feature representation cannot comprehen-sively describe intricate fault patterns, and distribution discrepancies across rotating speedsdegrade the cross-condition generalization of diagnostic models. To address these limita-tions, this paper proposes an adaptive domain-aligned multi-modal feature fusion network(ADAMFFN). Three parallel branches extract complementary features from dual-channelvibration signals: spatial coupling features from orbit images, time–frequency energy fea-tures from continuous wavelet transform (CWT) representations, and frequency-domainstatistical (FreqStat) features from power and envelope spectra. Heterogeneous features aremapped into a shared latent subspace through a unied projection layer, deep cross-modalinteraction is realized by a progressive fusion network, and a domain alignment mechanismbased on a domain-adversarial neural network (DANN) is introduced to eliminate source–target distribution gaps via adversarial training. On eight leave-one-speed-out (LOSO)cross-speed tasks constructed on the public WT-Planetary Gearbox dataset, ADAMFFNachieves an average accuracy of 99.25%, outperforming the best single-branch and dual-branch schemes by 2.63 and 0.40 percentage points, respectively; ablation experimentsverify the complementarity of the three modalities and the effectiveness of domain align-ment. Cross-condition external validation on the Southeast University (SEU) gearboxdataset further demonstrates its generalization capability under a different test rig andacquisition conditions.Keywords: planetary gearbox; fault diagnosis; multi-modal feature fusion; domain adver-sarial neural network; cross-speed condition; deep learning 1. IntroductionPlanetary gearboxes possess the merits of high power density, large transmissionratio and strong load-bearing capacity, which are widely deployed in critical equipmentincluding wind turbine generators, aero-engines and high-speed trains [1]. However,planetary gear transmissions also have inherent limitations: their structures are complex,demanding high manufacturing and assembly precision, and uneven load sharing amongplanet gears tends to induce dynamic imbalance [2]. Moreover, the compound motionsamong the sun gear, planet gears, ring gear, and carrier introduce complicated meshingmodulation and transmission-path effects, making fault-induced vibration characteristicsstrongly speed-dependent. These characteristics render planetary gearbox fault diagnosismore challenging in terms of signal coupling than that of xed-axis gearboxes. Operating Machines 2026, 14, 960 https://doi.org/10.3390/machines14090960 Machines 2026, 14, 960 2 of 23 under complex variable-speed and variable-load conditions over long-term service, keycomponents are prone to failures including wear, cracks and tooth breakage [3–7]. If suchfaults cannot be detected in a timely manner, they may lead to unplanned shutdowns andeconomic losses in mild cases, or trigger cascading safety accidents in severe cases. Accord-ingly, developing accurate and robust intelligent fault diagnosis technologies for planetarygearboxes has important theoretical signicance and engineering value for ensuring thesafe operation of equipment and reducing operation and maintenance costs.Early fault diagnosis research mainly relies on signal processing technologies, whichextract fault-sensitive features via time-domain statistical indicators, spectrum analysis,and time–frequency analysis tools [8–12]. Such methods achieve satisfactory performanceunder steady operating conditions, yet their diagnostic accuracy heavily depends on man-ual feature engineering and domain expertise, resulting in limited feature representationcapacity when processing strongly non-stationary and heavily modulated vibration signalsof planetary gearboxes [13,14]. In recent years, deep learning methods represented byconvolutional neural network (CNN), long short-term memory (LSTM) and Transformershave exhibited prominent superiority in gear and bearing fault diagnosis owing to theirend-to-end hierarchical feature learning capability [15–18]. Cross-condition diagnosticstrategies based on transfer learning have been further developed [19–21]. Nevertheless,most existing deep diagnostic models adopt single-modality input and assume that trainingand test data follow identical distributions, which gives rise to two critical drawbacks inpractical variable-speed scenarios. First, single feature representation shows inconsistentsensitivity to various fault types and suffers from information loss. Second, speed uctu-ations induce signal distribution shift, drastically degrading model performance underunseen working conditions.Multi-modal information fusion offers an effective solution to break through thebottleneck of single representation [22]. According to the fusion stage, fusion strategiescan be categorized into feature-level fusion, decision-level fusion and hybrid fusion. Liet al. [23] performed early fusion of vibration and acoustic emission signals to improve thediagnosis accuracy of gear faults. Wang et al. [24] introduced an attention mechanism toadaptively adjust the weight of each modality and strengthen the discriminability of fusedrepresentations. It is worth noting that most existing fusion studies focus on homogeneousfeatures collected from multi-sensor vibration signals. In contrast, heterogeneous featuresincluding images and vectors present obvious disparities in data type, dimension, scaleand distribution. Direct concatenation or weighted summation fails to achieve effectiveinformation complementarity, and may even degrade diagnostic performance due to cross-modality inconsistency [25]. Therefore, the deep fusion mechanism for such heterogeneousfeatures remains to be further investigated.Transfer learning and domain adaptation methods have attracted extensive attentionto tackle the issue of cross-condition distribution shift. A domain-adversarial neural net-work is proposed, which achieves adversarial alignment of feature distributions betweensource and target domains via a gradient reversal layer [26]. Tzeng et al. [27] furtherdeveloped an adversarial discriminative domain adaptation framework. Han et al. [28]incorporated DANN into cross-load fault diagnosis of bearings, which effectively narrowsthe distribution discrepancy induced by varying working conditions. Nevertheless, mostexisting domain adaptation studies are designed for single-modality features. How toembed a domain alignment mechanism into a multi-modal fusion framework such thatheterogeneous fused features simultaneously possess discriminability and domain invari-ance remains an open problem that has not been fully addressed. In recent years, noveldeep transfer learning architectures such as Transformers, contrastive and self-supervisedlearning, graph neural networks, and diffusion models have developed rapidly in faulthttps://doi.org/10.3390/machines14090960 Machines 2026, 14, 960 3 of 23 diagnosis [17,29]. It should be claried that the focus of this work is not to propose a newnetwork backbone, but to address the deep fusion and domain alignment of heterogeneousmulti-modal features under cross-speed conditions. These novel backbones are structurallyorthogonal to the proposed framework and can be integrated as alternative encoders forthe individual branches in future work.In summary, there are three major challenges in cross-speed fault diagnosis of plan-etary gearboxes: (1) single-type features fail to fully capture complex fault information;(2) effective deep fusion mechanisms for heterogeneous multi-modal features are lacking;(3) distribution shifts caused by speed variations degrade the generalization performanceof diagnostic models. To address the above challenges, this paper proposes an adaptivedomain-aligned multi-modal feature fusion network (ADAMFFN). The main contributionsare summarized as follows:(1) A tri-modal complementary feature extraction framework is proposed. Threeparallel branches corresponding to shaft orbit (Orbit), continuous wavelet transform (CWT)time–frequency images and frequency-domain statistics (FreqStat) are constructed to charac-terize the operating conditions of planetary gearboxes from three complementary perspec-tives, including spatial coupling, time–frequency energy and spectral statistics, achievingmulti-dimensional coverage of fault information.(2) A unied projection and progressive fusion mechanism for heterogeneous featuresis designed. The unied projection layer maps heterogeneous features with disparatedimensions and distributions into a shared subspace, and a three-layer fusion networkequipped with progressive dropout regularization is adopted to learn deep cross-modalinteractions, realizing collaborative enhancement of complementary features.(3) The DANN domain alignment mechanism is embedded into the multi-modal fusionframework. Adversarial training between source and target domains is performed on fusedfeatures via the gradient reversal layer, enabling the fused representations to simultaneouslypossess discriminability and domain invariance and improving the generalization capacityof the model under unseen speed conditions.(4) A systematic cross-speed verication framework is established. Eight leave-one-speed-out (LOSO) cross-condition diagnostic tasks are designed based on the WT-PlanetaryGearbox dataset, and systematic comparisons with six single-branch and dual-branchmethods are conducted. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed ADAMFFNachieves an average diagnostic accuracy of 99.25%. Ablation experiments further verifythe complementarity of each modality and the effectiveness of the domain alignmentmechanism. Moreover, cross-condition external validation on the SEU gearbox datasetfurther demonstrates that the generalization capability of the proposed method is notrestricted to a single test rig.It should be claried that the individual components (Orbit, CWT, FreqStat, andDANN) are not new in themselves. The novelty lies in their problem-oriented integrationinto a unied framework: heterogeneous features are made compatible through uniedprojection, fused progressively rather than simply concatenated, and domain-aligned onthe fused representation, jointly addressing heterogeneous feature incompatibility andspeed-induced domain shift in cross-speed planetary gearbox diagnosis. The ADAMFFNis built upon the closed-set assumption [30]; the recognition of unknown faults is beyondthe scope of this work and is discussed in the conclusion section.The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Theoretical Background 2.1. Orbit Diagram AnalysisThe orbit diagram serves as a vital visual analysis tool for fault diagnosis of rotatingmachinery. By plotting the motion trajectory of the rotor center within the plane perpen-dicular to the rotating shaft, it intuitively reects the spatial motion behavior of the rotorand globally reveals the dynamic characteristics of rotary equipment, thus forming animportant type of diagnostic feature [31].Assume that two vibration sensors are mounted along mutually perpendicular di-rections on the rotating machinery, and the collected vibration signals are denoted as x(t)and y(t), respectively. The orbit diagram is generated by plotting a parametric curve on atwo-dimensional plane with x(t) as the horizontal axis and y(t) as the vertical axis, and itsmathematical formulation is given: r(t) = [ x(t), y(t) ] T , t 2 [ 0, T ] , (1)where r(t) represents the position vector of the rotor center at time t, and T denotes the totalsampling duration.Distinct geometric patterns can be observed on orbit diagrams under different faultconditions. Under healthy operating conditions, the orbit diagram appears as a smoothapproximate circle or ellipse [31]. For missing-tooth and broken-tooth faults, the trajectoryexhibits petal shapes, local sharp corners and double-layer nested structures. In the case ofgear tooth wear, the orbit presents an ellipse covered with uniform ripples. Such differencesin geometric morphology provide a physical basis for fault classication based on orbitdiagram images [32].Nevertheless, planet gears inside planetary gearboxes rotate about their own axeswhile revolving around the sun gear, introducing prominent modulation effects in vibrationsignals. The corresponding orbit diagrams generally contain folds, nested contours andperiodic distortions induced by sideband modulation [33]. Effective fault informationcannot be accurately extracted merely through manual observation or handcrafted featuredesign. Therefore, deep learning is adopted in this paper to mine latent fault informationcontained in orbit diagrams. 2.2. Continuous Wavelet TransformThe continuous wavelet transform (CWT) provides an effective framework for charac-terizing non-stationary signals jointly in the time and frequency domains. By introducingwavelet basis functions with variable scales, it enables multi-resolution analysis of sig-nals. CWT possesses adaptive time–frequency resolution: it achieves high time resolutionfor high-frequency components and high frequency resolution for low-frequency compo-nents, which makes it highly suitable for analyzing non-stationary mechanical vibrationsignals [34]. Frequency-Domain Statistical FeaturesFrequency-domain analysis is a classical technique for mechanical fault diagnosis. Byanalyzing the frequency components and their statistical properties of signals, periodicfaults such as gear meshing faults can be effectively identied [35]. Two frequency-domainanalysis approaches are mainly adopted in this work, namely power spectral densityanalysis and envelope spectrum analysis.Power spectral density (PSD) characterizes the distribution of signal power over thefrequency domain. The Welch algorithm is employed to estimate the PSD, which canefciently reduce the variance of spectral estimation. The Welch method divides the signalx(t) into K segments, with each segment containing M sampling points, applies windowingto each segment, and averages the periodograms of all segments. Tri-Modal Feature Extraction 3.2.1. Orbit Image GenerationThe Orbit branch is designed to extract spatial coupling features from dual-channelvibration signals. Its detailed processing procedures are presented as follows:Step 1: Data normalization: Min–max normalization is performed on dual-channelvibration signals to scale signal amplitudes into the range of [0, 1], eliminating the inuenceof dimensional differences on trajectory shapes.Step 2: Two-dimensional histogram construction: The normalized signals are mappedonto a 128128 two-dimensional grid, and the data point frequency within each gridcell is counted to construct a 2D histogram H2R(128  128). To improve the separabilityof features, the single-channel histogram is replicated into three channels to generate ared–green–blue (RGB) image with the size of 128  128  3. https://doi.org/10.3390/machines14090960 Machines 2026, 14, 960 7 of 23 Step 3: Gaussian smoothing: A 33 Gaussian lter is applied to the two-dimensionalhistogram to smooth noisy points while preserving the overall morphological features oftrajectories. The processed shaft orbit diagrams corresponding to various fault conditionsare shown in Figure 2. Figure 2. Shaft orbit diagrams under different operating conditions.Step 4: Feature extraction network: A ResNet-like architecture is adopted to processorbit images. The network starts with a 77 convolutional layer with 64 lters and astride of 2. After batch normalization, rectied linear unit (ReLU) activation and 33max pooling with stride 2, the spatial resolution is reduced from 128128 to 3232.Three residual blocks are stacked subsequently: the rst residual block retains 64 lters andutilizes an identity shortcut; the second and third residual blocks double the number oflters to 128 and 256 in sequence, and adopt 11 projection shortcuts to match dimensionvariations. Finally, global average pooling is implemented to output a 256-dimensionalfeature vector. 3.2.2. CWT Time–Frequency Image GenerationThe CWT branch aims to extract time–frequency energy features from non-stationaryvibration signals, and its detailed processing steps are given as follows:Step 1: Continuous wavelet transform: The Morlet wavelet is selected as the basisfunction to perform CWT on dual-channel vibration signals, and the frequency range isset to cover the major frequency bands corresponding to characteristic fault frequencies ofplanetary gearboxes. The output of CWT is a complex matrix of time–frequency coefcients.Step 2: Magnitude calculation and logarithmic compression: The magnitudes ofCWT coefcients are calculated to derive the time–frequency amplitude representation.Logarithmic compression is then applied to boost the visibility of low-energy ne detailswhile compressing the dynamic range of high-energy regions.Step 3: Resizing: The logarithmically compressed time–frequency representation isresized to a standard dimension of 64641 for subsequent processing by the CNN. Thehttps://doi.org/10.3390/machines14090960 Machines 2026, 14, 960 8 of 23 processed CWT time–frequency maps of different operating conditions are illustrated inFigure 3. Figure 3. CWT time–frequency images of different operating conditions.Step 4: Feature extraction network. A lightweight CNN architecture is designed toprocess CWT time–frequency images. The network consists of three convolutional blocks,each composed of a convolutional layer, batch normalization and ReLU activation, followedby 22 max pooling after the rst two blocks. The rst convolutional block adopts alarge 55 kernel with 32 lters to capture coarse-grained time–frequency patterns. Thesubsequent two blocks utilize 33 kernels, with the number of lters increased to 64and 128 sequentially for extracting ner time–frequency features. The two pooling layersreduce the spatial size from 6464 to 1616. Considering the translation invarianceof time–frequency representations, residual connections are not deployed in this branch.Finally, global average pooling is performed to output a 128-dimensional feature vector. 3.2.3. Frequency-Domain Statistical Feature VectorThe FreqStat branch is designed to extract frequency-domain statistical regularitiesand characterize fault features from the frequency perspective. Its specic processingprocedures are detailed as follows:Step 1: Welch power spectrum estimation: The Welch power spectral density estima-tion is implemented on vibration signals with a 512-point Hamming window and 50%overlap. The power spectrum covering the main characteristic frequency range is calculated.After logarithmic compression, 32 points are uniformly sampled to form a 32-dimensionallogarithmic power spectral feature.Step 2: Envelope spectrum analysis: The Hilbert transform is applied to vibrationsignals to extract the amplitude envelope. The same Welch spectral estimation is carried outon the envelope signal to compute the envelope spectrum within the main characteristicfrequency band. Each convolutional block includesconvolution, batch normalization, ReLU activation and max pooling. The resolution alongthe frequency axis is gradually reduced from 64 to 32 and then to 16. The number oflters increases sequentially from 32 to 64 and 128 to realize hierarchical abstraction offrequency-domain information. Feature Projection and FusionThere are discrepancies in dimension and data distribution among the features outputby the three branches. A projection layer is introduced to standardize these features, wherefully connected layers project the feature vectors of each branch into a unied dimension.After projection processing, each branch outputs a 128-dimensional feature vector, whichunies the dimensions of heterogeneous features.After unied projection, the three 128-dimensional feature vectors are concatenatedalong the channel dimension to form a 384-dimensional fused vector. This fused vectorsimultaneously integrates spatial trajectory information (Orbit), time–frequency energydistribution information (CWT), and frequency-domain statistical regularity information(FreqStat), achieving comprehensive coverage of fault information from multiple perspec-tives. DANN-Based Domain Alignment MechanismIn variable-speed fault diagnosis scenarios, the distribution of vibration signals variessignicantly under different rotating speeds. Such distribution shift leads to performancedegradation of the model trained on source domains (training speeds) when deployed ontarget domains (testing speeds). For each task, one rotational speed is treated as the targetdomain (test set), while the remaining seven speeds serve as the source domain (trainingset + validation set). The detailed partition of the eight tasks is listed in Table 2.In each task, the source-domain data are split into a training set and a validationset at a ratio of 3:1. ConclusionsTo address the variable-speed fault diagnosis problem of planetary gearboxes,this paper proposes an adaptive domain-aligned multi-modal feature fusion network(ADAMFFN). The developed method comprehensively characterizes the operating condi-tions of planetary gearboxes from multiple complementary perspectives via three parallelheterogeneous feature extraction branches: the Orbit spatial trajectory branch, the CWTtime–frequency branch, and the FreqStat frequency-domain statistics branch. A uniedprojection layer and progressive fusion network are employed to realize effective fusionof heterogeneous features. Furthermore, a DANN-based domain alignment mechanismreduces the distribution discrepancy between training source domains and testing targetdomains, remarkably improving the generalization ability of the model on unseen rotatingspeeds. Experimental results of eight LOSO cross-speed fault diagnosis tasks conducted onthe WT-Planetary Gearbox dataset demonstrate the following conclusions:(1) The proposed ADAMFFN achieves an average accuracy of 99.25%, outperformingall single-branch and dual-branch comparative methods, and maintains 98.45% even underthe most challenging 55 Hz condition.(2) Ablation experiments verify the complementarity of the three feature modalities,and full three-branch fusion achieves superior performance over any single-branch ordual-branch combination.(3) The DANN domain alignment mechanism improves the average accuracy byapproximately two percentage points, effectively addressing the distribution shift problemunder variable-speed working conditions.(4) In the cross-condition external validation on the SEU gearbox dataset, ADAMFFNachieves an average accuracy of 94.54%, demonstrating that its generalization capability isnot restricted to a single test rig or acquisition system.Although the proposed method achieves outstanding performance on cross-speedfault diagnosis tasks, several limitations remain and will be addressed in future work. First,vibration signals in practical industrial environments are often contaminated by strongnoise; preprocessing modules based on denoising autoencoders or signal augmentationnetworks will be investigated to enhance diagnostic performance under low signal-to-noiseratio conditions. Second, the method relies on the closed-set assumption, under whichunknown faults may be forcibly assigned to known categories; prototype-based rejection oruncertainty estimation will be introduced into the fused feature space, drawing on open-setsemi-supervised contrastive learning, to enable the recognition of novel faults. Third, theeight LOSO tasks are constructed at discrete steady-state speeds; angular resampling andorder-tracking modules will be introduced at the front end of ADAMFFN to improve itsadaptability under continuously varying-speed conditions such as startup and shutdown.Author Contributions: Methodology, X.X.; software, X.W.; formal analysis, X.X.; data curation,X.X.; writing—original draft, X.X.
